# Thumbnail / cover prompt (9:16): Pearl Harbor Was on Radar 46 Minutes Early (1941)

Same style as your Mincemeat reference: cinematic movie poster, one face, huge text. Set **9:16 (portrait)**.

## Prompt A (recommended)
```
Create a vertical 9:16 YouTube Shorts cover, hyper-realistic cinematic movie-poster style, dark, high contrast, sharp detail.

Centre: a young American army private in 1941, short hair, khaki shirt, wearing bulky old radio headphones, his face lit bright green from below by a round glowing radar oscilloscope screen, eyes wide with alarm, looking straight at the camera.

Behind him, through the open door of a dark army radar truck: a dawn sky over the ocean, with a dark swarm of tiny aircraft silhouettes approaching from the horizon.

Text, perfectly spelled, bold condensed capitals with a thick black outline:
- Top, huge, white: "NOT TO"
- Under it, huge, glowing red: "WORRY"
- Bottom, small, green radar-glow label: "7:02 AM · PEARL HARBOR"

Keep his face completely free of text. No other words, no watermark, no logo, no modern objects.
```

## Prompt B: short version
```
9:16 cinematic poster photo: a young 1941 US Army radar operator with headphones, face lit green by a round radar screen, eyes wide; behind him through a truck door, dawn sky with a swarm of distant aircraft. Text: "NOT TO" (white) "WORRY" (red), small green "7:02 AM · PEARL HARBOR". Only these words, spelled exactly. No watermark.
```

## Negative prompt (if supported)
```
misspelled text, extra words, gibberish letters, watermark, logo, modern clothing, modern headset, cartoon, painting style, blurry, deformed hands, extra fingers, gore
```

## Tips
- **Why "NOT TO WORRY":** it's the twist the title doesn't give away. There are no quote marks because the record paraphrases the officer's words; it isn't a verbatim quote.
- In the Shorts editor you can instead pick a cover frame from the video: the first second (the operator in green light, "PEARL HARBOR · 7:02 AM").
- If a word is misspelled, reply: *"Keep everything the same, only fix the text to read exactly: NOT TO WORRY"*.
