## Accuracy notes (Wikipedia, *Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson*, citing Hibbert 1994 and Beatty 1807)
- **The shot:** a musket ball from the mizzen-top of the *Redoutable* at about 50 ft. It entered his left shoulder, passed through a lung and his spine.
- **His death:** about 4:30 pm, roughly three hours after being shot, aged 47.
- **The cask:** his body was placed in "a cask of brandy mixed with camphor and myrrh", lashed to Victory's mainmast and placed under guard.
- **Gibraltar:** at Gibraltar the body was moved to a lead-lined coffin filled with spirits of wine.
- **"Tapping the admiral" / "Nelson's Blood":** a sailors' legend. The narration says "Legend says… There's no proof."
- **The coffin:** made from the mast of *L'Orient* (blown up at the Nile, 1798), an ironic gift from Captain Hallowell.
- **The funeral (9 January 1806):** 32 admirals, over 100 captains and 10,000 soldiers. He lies in a sarcophagus originally carved for Cardinal Wolsey.

## Description
```
Britain's greatest admiral came home from Trafalgar in a barrel of brandy.

On 21 October 1805 Admiral Horatio Nelson was shot by a French marksman from the rigging of the Redoutable. The ball went through his shoulder, lung and spine, and he died three hours later as the battle was won. With England weeks away, his surgeon placed his body in a cask of brandy mixed with camphor and myrrh, lashed it to HMS Victory's mainmast and put it under armed guard. At Gibraltar he was moved to a lead coffin filled with spirits of wine.

Legend says sailors secretly drank from the cask, "tapping the admiral", and that's why rum is called "Nelson's Blood". There's no proof, and it wasn't even rum.

His outer coffin was made from the mast of L'Orient, the French flagship he destroyed at the Nile. On 9 January 1806, 32 admirals, over 100 captains and 10,000 soldiers escorted him to St Paul's, where he lies in a sarcophagus first carved for Cardinal Wolsey.
```
