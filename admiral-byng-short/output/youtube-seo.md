## Accuracy notes (Wikipedia, *John Byng*, and its cited works)
- **The mission:** ordered in 1756 to relieve Minorca. The fleet was hastily assembled, some ships were in poor condition, and he sailed still more than 800 men short. The narration says "hundreds of sailors short", which is a safe understatement.
- **The battle:** Battle of Minorca, 20 May 1756. The fleet was damaged; a war council voted to return to Gibraltar, and Fort St Philip surrendered on 29 June.
- **The report:** on 26 June 1756 the *London Gazette* printed an edited version of his report that made him look like a coward. Effigies were burned.
- **The court martial:** acquitted of cowardice, but found to have "not done his utmost" under the 12th Article of War, which carried a mandatory death sentence. The court unanimously recommended mercy, and the King refused it.
- **The execution:** 14 March 1757, on the quarterdeck of HMS Monarch in the Solent, with men from the fleet watching. He knelt on a cushion, dropped his handkerchief, and a squad of Marines fired.
- **Voltaire:** *Candide* (1759): "il est bon de tuer de temps en temps un amiral pour encourager les autres".
- **The pardon:** in 2007 his descendants petitioned for a pardon and the Ministry of Defence refused.
- **The epitaph** at Southill includes "fell a martyr to political persecution".

## Description
```
Britain shot one of its own admirals in 1757, even though his judges asked for mercy.

In 1756 Admiral John Byng was sent to save Minorca from the French with a fleet in poor repair and hundreds of sailors short. After a battle off the island he sailed to Gibraltar for repairs, and Minorca fell. In London the government printed an edited version of his report that made him look like a coward, and crowds burned him in effigy.

His court martial cleared him of cowardice but ruled he had not "done his utmost". Under the Articles of War, that meant death. The court asked the King for mercy. The King refused. On 14 March 1757, on the deck of HMS Monarch, Byng knelt on a cushion, dropped his handkerchief, and the Marines fired.

Voltaire mocked it in Candide: in this country it is good to kill an admiral from time to time "to encourage the others". In 2007 his family asked for a pardon. It was refused.
```
