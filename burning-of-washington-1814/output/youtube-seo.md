## Description

```
In August 1814, British troops burned Washington, D.C. — the Capitol, the President's House, the Treasury. Less than a day later, a violent storm and a tornado hit the city.

Did the storm really save Washington? Here's what happened in the 26 hours Britain held the U.S. capital.

⏱ What's inside
• Aug 24, 1814: 4,500 British troops rout 6,000 Americans at Bladensburg, the "Bladensburg Races"
• The Capitol burns, and the 3,000-book Library of Congress with it
• British troops eat President Madison's dinner, then burn his house
• Aug 25: a thunderstorm (possibly a hurricane) and a tornado hit the city
• "Is this the weather to which you are accustomed in this infernal country?" (Adm. George Cockburn)
• Three weeks later: Baltimore, Fort McHenry and "The Star-Spangled Banner"
• Myth: the White House was NOT painted white to hide the scorch marks (it was whitewashed in 1798)
```
