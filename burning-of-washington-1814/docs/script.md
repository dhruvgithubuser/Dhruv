# A Storm Hit Washington the Day After Britain Burned It

**Format:** YouTube Short / Instagram Reel, 9:16, about 65 s
**Style:** Vox-style explainer. Real archival engravings and portraits used as cut-out collage, with yellow highlighter, hand-drawn circles and arrows, a flat vector map, kinetic type and data graphics. **No AI paintings.**
**Voice:** deep American documentary male, calm and explanatory.

---

## Research notes (fact sheet)

| Fact | Value used | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Battle of Bladensburg | 24 Aug 1814. British about 4,370–4,500; US about 6,000–7,000. US rout nicknamed the "Bladensburg Races". | Wikipedia: *Battle of Bladensburg* (Pitch 1998; Borneman) |
| Entering Washington | The evening of 24 Aug, about 4,500 troops under Maj. Gen. Robert Ross and R. Adm. George Cockburn. Madison had fled (that night he was at Brookeville, MD). | *Burning of Washington* |
| The Capitol | Burned first, south wing then north. The Library of Congress (about 3,000 volumes) was destroyed. Loss: $787,163.28. | *Burning of Washington* (Allen, *History of the U.S. Capitol*) |
| The White House | Dinner prepared for the President's party was eaten by the British (Capt. Blanshard's sappers), then the house was burned. Fires were fed overnight. | Paul Jennings, *A Colored Man's Reminiscences of James Madison* (1865); *Burning of Washington* |
| Other buildings | The Treasury and War Office were burned. The Patent Office was saved by William Thornton. Americans burned their own Navy Yard and the 44-gun USS *Columbia*. | *Burning of Washington* |
| The storm | The afternoon of 25 Aug: a very heavy thunderstorm (possibly a hurricane) put out the fires. A tornado touched down near Constitution Ave, lifted two cannons and dropped them yards away, and killed British troops and civilians. | NWS Sterling, VA, "D.C. Tornado Events"; *Burning of Washington* |
| Cockburn quote | "Dear God! Is this the weather to which you are accustomed in this infernal country?" (reported exchange) | *Burning of Washington* (Pitch, *The Burning of Washington*) |
| Occupation length | About 26 hours. The British withdrew on the night of 25 Aug. | *Burning of Washington* |
| Historians' view | Ross only meant to destroy public buildings, not to hold the city, so the storm didn't "save" it. The rain also cracked the charred walls. | *Burning of Washington* |
| Baltimore | 12–14 Sept 1814. Ross was killed at North Point, Fort McHenry held through about 25 hours of bombardment, and Francis Scott Key wrote "Defence of Fort M'Henry", which became "The Star-Spangled Banner" (anthem since 1931). | *Battle of Baltimore* |
| White House myth | The house wasn't painted white to cover the scorch marks: it was first whitewashed in 1798. T. Roosevelt made "White House" the official name in 1901. | White House Historical Association |

## Script (13 beats)

| # | Narration | On screen (Vox treatment) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In 1814, Britain burned Washington. Less than a day later, the sky put the fire out. | 1876 engraving *Burning of Washington* cut out on off-white paper. Yellow highlighter swipe on "BURNED"; a rain-cloud icon slides in and wipes the flames. |
| 2 | August 24th, 1814. About forty-five hundred British troops crushed an American force of six thousand at Bladensburg, Maryland. The rout was so fast it was mocked as "the Bladensburg Races." | Flat vector map: Chesapeake Bay → Benedict → Bladensburg → D.C. Red arrow. Unit bars 4,500 vs 6,000. Running-figure icons and a highlighted label "THE BLADENSBURG RACES". |
| 3 | That evening, they marched into the capital. President Madison had already fled. | Madison portrait cut-out with a "FLED" stamp; a dotted arrow to Brookeville, MD. |
| 4 | They torched the Capitol first. The Library of Congress, all three thousand books, burned with it. | Capitol line-drawing with flame icons. A grid of 3,000 book glyphs burns away, with a counter. |
| 5 | Then they went up Pennsylvania Avenue to the President's House. Dinner was still on the table. British troops ate it, then set the house on fire. | City-plan map with a route along Pennsylvania Ave. Plate, fork and knife icons tick off; Paul Jennings' memoir quote card is highlighted. |
| 6 | They fed the fires all night, and burned the Treasury and the War Office too. | Night map: a fire icon pops on each building, with a checklist of buildings. |
| 7 | The next afternoon, the sky turned black. A violent storm hit the city, and the rain drowned the flames. | Weather-radar motion graphic: cloud cells sweep the map, rain lines, flame icons go out one by one. |
| 8 | A tornado tore through the center of town, lifting cannons off the ground and killing British soldiers and civilians alike. | Vector tornado funnel moves across the map; cannon icons are lifted and dropped; "NWS: D.C. tornado, Aug 25 1814" source tag. |
| 9 | Admiral George Cockburn reportedly asked: "Is this the weather to which you are accustomed in this infernal country?" | The Halls portrait of Cockburn (Washington burning behind him) with a typed quote card and highlighted "INFERNAL COUNTRY". |
| 10 | That night, the British marched out. The occupation had lasted about twenty-six hours. | Clock counter 0 → 26 h; the red arrow retreats off the map. |
| 11 | So did a storm save Washington? Most historians say no. The British came to burn, not to stay. And the rain cracked the scorched walls even further. | Big question mark, then a split card: "MYTH: storm saved the city" vs "REALITY: a 26-hour raid". A crack graphic runs across the Munger White House ruin. |
| 12 | Three weeks later, the same army attacked Baltimore and failed. Watching the flag survive, Francis Scott Key wrote the poem that became the Star-Spangled Banner. | Map pans to Baltimore. Fort McHenry engraving cut-out, a circled flag and the lyric line typed. |
| 13 | And no, the White House wasn't painted white to hide the scorch marks. It had been white since 1798. | Munger's burned President's House with a "MYTH" stamp. Timeline 1798 → 1814 → 1901. End card. |
