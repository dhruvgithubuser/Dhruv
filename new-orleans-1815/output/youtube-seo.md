## 3. Description

```
The Battle of New Orleans (January 8, 1815) was fought two weeks AFTER the peace treaty ending the War of 1812 was signed — because the news was still crossing the Atlantic by ship.

On December 24, 1814, the Treaty of Ghent was signed in Belgium. With no one in America aware of it, about 8,000 British veterans under General Sir Edward Pakenham attacked Andrew Jackson's line at Chalmette, five miles below New Orleans. In just over 30 minutes the British lost 2,037 killed, wounded or captured — Pakenham among the dead. American casualties: 71.

The treaty took effect on February 17, 1815. The battle changed nothing about the peace, but it made Andrew Jackson a national hero and, in 1829, the 7th President of the United States.
```
