## Description (paste as is)
```
22 January 1879. At Isandlwana, in the Zulu Kingdom, the British Army lost over 1,300 men in a single afternoon, to an army armed mostly with spears and shields. This is the battle told from the side of the man who planned it: the Zulu commander Ntshingwayo kaMahole Khoza.

Why it happened: in December 1878, Sir Bartle Frere sent King Cetshwayo an ultimatum, without London's approval, demanding among other things that the Zulu army be disbanded. When it expired, Lord Chelmsford invaded Zululand on 11 January 1879.

What happened: Ntshingwayo's army of around 20,000 marched more than 50 miles in five days and hid in the Ngwebeni Valley, about seven miles from the British camp. On the morning of the 22nd, Chelmsford marched out with about 2,800 men, leaving just over 1,300 at Isandlwana. Discovered a day early, the Zulu regiments attacked on the run in the "horns and chest of the buffalo". Through an extended firing line, the ammunition debate and a solar eclipse at 2:30 PM, the camp fell by 3:30.

The aftermath: Rorke's Drift that night, Ulundi in July, Cetshwayo's capture, and Ntshingwayo's death in 1883.
```
