How did the French and Indian War start? In May 1754, a 22-year-old George Washington led a 15-minute ambush at Jumonville Glen that lit the fuse of a world war.

This animated documentary follows the young Virginian from his 1753 winter mission to Fort Le Boeuf, through the race for the Forks of the Ohio, the dawn ambush that killed Ensign Joseph Coulon de Jumonville, the disputed story of how Jumonville died, and the surrender at Fort Necessity, where Washington signed a French document admitting to an "assassination." From there: the Seven Years' War across five continents, the end of French North America, and the taxes that led to the American Revolution.

## Fact notes
- Battle length "about a quarter of an hour", casualties (Jumonville + 9 killed, 21 captured, 1 Virginian killed): Washington's diary, as quoted
  on Wikipedia. Other accounts give 13–14 dead.
- Jumonville's death: the French report (shot while the summons was read) and the Shaw/Kaninguen accounts (Tanacharison's hatchet) are both presented.
- The capitulation's French text reads "venger l'assassin…"; the narration says "the French for assassination".
- Fort Necessity: 31 killed, 70 wounded of ≈400 ("about one in four").
- Walpole's "set the world on fire" line is the standard attribution (Horace Walpole).
