## Description
```
21 October 1805: 27 British ships of the line attacked 33 French and Spanish warships off Cape Trafalgar. By sunset, Britain had lost none. This is the Battle of Trafalgar: what happened, and why it happened at all.

Why it happened: Napoleon needed the English Channel to invade Britain. His plan sent Vice-Admiral Villeneuve across the Atlantic to lure the Royal Navy away, but Villeneuve turned south to Cádiz instead of Brest, and on 25 August 1805 Napoleon's invasion army marched away to fight Austria. Trafalgar did not stop the invasion; it decided something bigger.

What happened: Nelson's two columns sailed straight at the enemy line, cut it into three and destroyed the centre and rear. "England expects that every man will do his duty." HMS Victory locked with the Redoutable, a French marksman shot Nelson, and he died at half past four, as the victory was completed. Then a week-long storm wrecked the prizes.

The result: Britain lost no ships; France and Spain lost 18 ships captured or destroyed and around 4,400 killed. Napoleon won at Austerlitz six weeks later, but Trafalgar settled who ruled the sea for the rest of the century.

Was Trafalgar won by Nelson's genius, or by Villeneuve's fear? Tell us below.
```
