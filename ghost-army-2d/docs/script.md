# The Ghost Army: 2D WWII documentary Short (narration script)

9:16, about 104 s, deep American narrator. Written for a US audience, so it explains what a Sherman was, how German intelligence watched the front, and how each Ghost Army trick defeated one of those channels.

| # | Narration |
|---|---|
| 1 | In the summer of 1944, two Frenchmen on bicycles stopped in shock. Four American soldiers had just picked up a Sherman tank. |
| 2 | The Sherman was America's main battle tank: 33 tons of steel. |
| 3 | One soldier reportedly shrugged: "Americans are very strong." |
| 4 | The truth? The tank was rubber. And it weighed 93 pounds. |
| 5 | It belonged to one of the most secret units of World War II: the 23rd Headquarters Special Troops. The Ghost Army. |
| 6 | 1,100 men, many of them artists, designers and ad men from New York and Philadelphia. Their mission: make the Germans believe they were 30,000. |
| 7 | German intelligence watched the front three ways: from the air, over the radio, and through spies. The Ghost Army faked all three. |
| 8 | From the air: inflatable tanks, trucks and artillery, set up overnight. A bulldozer cut fake tank tracks, so the scene looked real in reconnaissance photos. |
| 9 | Over the radio: operators copied the traffic of real divisions, down to each operator's style in Morse code. |
| 10 | And on the ground: speakers on half-tracks played recordings of tank columns, heard up to 15 miles away. Soldiers wore fake unit patches and talked loosely in cafés where spies might be listening. |
| 11 | From Normandy to the Rhine, they staged more than twenty deceptions. |
| 12 | Their biggest came in March 1945. To cover the Ninth Army's crossing of the Rhine, they faked two entire infantry divisions with more than 600 inflatable dummies. |
| 13 | The Germans took the bait. The real crossing, to the north, met little resistance. |
| 14 | The Army kept it all classified for more than fifty years, until 1996. |
| 15 | In 2022, Congress awarded the Ghost Army the Congressional Gold Medal, for fighting with rubber, sound and imagination. |
| 16 | Would you have fallen for it? |

## Facts and sources

The fact sheet is in `../ghost-army-1944/docs/script.md`. Additional facts used here:
- Fake tank tracks cut by a bulldozer, so dummies looked real in reconnaissance photos (99% Invisible, "Show of Force").
- Radio operators imitated specific operators' Morse keying (99% Invisible / WBEZ).
- Fake division patches, stenciled bumper markings, and loose talk in cafés where enemy agents might overhear (Slate on Beyer & Sayles; Works That Work; HistoryNet).
- A real M4 Sherman weighs about 33 short tons (about 66,000 lb). The dummy weighed 93 lb.

Hedged wording: "reportedly" for the cyclist quote, and "might be listening" for the cafés. The unverified lives-saved figure is left out.
