# A. Historical research and fact-checking notes

The film presents the Pompeii story without inventing people, quotations or casualty figures. Where the evidence is uncertain, the narration hedges ("about", "may have", "studies suggest") and the picture is labelled (see "Honesty labels" below).

## Core facts used in the narration

| Claim in the film | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Pompeii was a prosperous town in Campania near the Bay of Naples | Established | Pompeii Archaeological Park; UNESCO WHC 829 |
| A major earthquake struck in **about AD 62** and damaged Pompeii and nearby towns | Established. Exact year debated (Seneca: 5 Feb AD 62; Tacitus: AD 63) | Seneca, *Naturales Quaestiones* 6.1; Tacitus, *Annals* 15.22. The narration says "about AD 62" |
| Repairs were still under way in AD 79 | Established (unfinished repairs and building work in several excavated buildings) | Excavation reports; summarised in Beard 2008 |
| The eruption was in **AD 79** | Established | Pliny the Younger, *Letters* 6.16, 6.20; Cassius Dio 66.21-24 |
| The traditional date is **24 August** | This is the reading of the manuscript tradition of Pliny's letter, and the manuscripts disagree | The narration says "copies of an ancient letter traditionally place it..." |
| An **autumn** date is possible | Supported by a charcoal inscription found in Regio V in 2018, read as *XVI K NOV* (17 October), plus debated clues (autumn fruits, sealed wine jars, braziers). Not certain: the inscription's year is not stated | Pompeii Archaeological Park announcement, Oct 2018; discussion in Doronzo et al. 2022 |
| A Plinian eruption column rose high above the mountain | Established. Modern reconstructions estimate a column of roughly 15-30+ km over the course of the eruption. The film shows "towering" and does not narrate a figure | Sigurdsson et al. 1982, 1985 |
| Pliny the Younger watched from Misenum and compared the cloud to an umbrella pine | Established (paraphrased, not quoted) | Pliny, *Letters* 6.16 |
| Pumice and ash fell first and accumulated over many hours; roofs collapsed under the load | Established. Fall deposits at Pompeii are about 2.5-3 m thick | Sigurdsson et al. 1985; Luongo, Perrotta & Scarpati 2003 |
| The eruption continued through the day and into the night | Established | Pliny 6.20; deposit stratigraphy |
| Pyroclastic density currents (not lava) followed when the column became unstable | Established | Sigurdsson et al. 1985; Doronzo et al. 2022 |
| Herculaneum was struck first; Pompeii was reached the next morning | Consensus sequence from deposit studies (Herculaneum by surges during the night; Pompeii by later currents in the morning of day 2). Exact hours debated, so the film gives no clock times | Sigurdsson et al. 1985; Luongo et al. 2003; Mastrolorenzo et al. 2010 |
| Many died; others escaped | Established. **No number is given in the film.** Remains of more than 1,000 people have been recovered at Pompeii; the total is unknown because part of the city and its surroundings are unexcavated and many died outside the walls | Pompeii Archaeological Park; Luongo et al. 2003 |
| Herculaneum, Oplontis and Stabiae were also affected | Established | UNESCO WHC 829; excavations |
| Pompeii disappeared from everyday knowledge but was preserved | Established. The area was known locally as *Civita*. The town was not identified by name until an inscription was found in 1763 | Excavation history |
| Vesuvius erupted again many times (e.g. 472, 1631) | Established | Global Volcanism Program, Vesuvius record |
| Digging at Pompeii began in 1748 | Established (Bourbon excavations under Charles of Bourbon; Herculaneum from 1738) | Excavation history |
| Giuseppe Fiorelli introduced more systematic methods in the 19th century | Established (director from 1863: excavation by regions and insulae, house numbering, plaster casts of voids) | Excavation history; the engraving used is captioned with his name |
| UNESCO World Heritage Site (1997), with Herculaneum and Torre Annunziata | Established | UNESCO WHC 829 |
| About two-thirds of the walled city has been excavated | Established (about 44 of roughly 66 ha) | Pompeii Archaeological Park |

## Deliberate visual decisions for accuracy

- **Vesuvius before AD 79 is drawn as a single, partly green cone.** The Bacchus-and-Vesuvius painting from the House of the Centenary (IX.8.3, now in the MANN, Naples) shows a single-peaked, vine-covered mountain. The modern twin profile (Monte Somma rim plus Gran Cono) appears only in the "centuries pass" and present-day shots.
- **No lava at Pompeii.** The danger is shown as pumice and ash fall (Scenes 5-6), then ground-hugging pyroclastic currents (Scene 7), with an explicit "lava vs pyroclastic current" comparison.
- **The phases are separate on screen.** First the column and fall (light grey to dark), then night and roof collapse, then the currents at dawn. The 6A timeline gives approximate phases, not hours.
- **Victims are never shown.** The emotional beat is an empty street and a child's wooden toy (Scene 7F). The forum caption reads: "Many died. Others escaped. The exact number of victims is unknown."
- **Coastline.** In AD 79 the shore near Pompeii lay closer to the town than it does today. The bay map is hand-plotted and labelled "approximate AD 79 coastline".
- **Architecture and dress.** The film shows two-storey frontages, tabernae with wooden shutter boards, a thermopolium with dolia set in the counter, basalt paving with ruts and stepping stones, painted electoral notices (shown as abstract strokes, not invented Latin), Fourth-Style red panels, a lararium, tunics, stolae, pallae and sandals. The town layout in the aerial shots is simplified and is not a survey.
- **The excavation scenes** use 18th-century dress (tricorn hats, coats) for 1748, and two public-domain 19th-century archival images for the Fiorelli era.

## Fictional elements (always labelled)

Marcus, Livia, Julia and Gaius are **fictional**. They carry the narrative and do not represent documented individuals. They have no dialogue, and their fate is left unknown. The Misenum onlooker is anonymous and is not presented as a portrait of Pliny.
