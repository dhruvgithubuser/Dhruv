Takeaways:
1. **Ravelin's formula:** an American underdog, one clever tactic, a number in the title, a date tag, 20–30 min, maps.
   - Titles: "The Genius Trap That…", "How 400 Americans Annihilated…", "The Mistake That Cost Britain…".
2. **Ravelin is fatiguing:** its last 10 Revolution videos mostly got 4–17K (down from 100K+). The audience wants the same
   formula on **new wars**. The War of 1812, the French & Indian War, the Barbary Wars, Texas, the Civil War and US WWII are
   almost untouched by it.
3. **Demand check (YouTube search, 5 min+ videos):**
   - Highest demand: Pearl Harbor and Midway (≈2.8M median of the top 10), Iwo Jima (1.2M), Wake Island (top 17M), Vicksburg (top 6.3M), Little Bighorn and the Alamo (~0.5–0.6M).
   - Wide-open gaps (few good videos): New Orleans 1815, the burning of Washington, Braddock's defeat, Barbary/Derna, San Jacinto, Chapultepec.
4. **RPM, honestly:** RPM is private. US military history earns mid-to-good RPM (an older US male audience). Shorts pay far less,
   so Shorts here are the funnel and **the long videos are the money**. Aim for 20–25 min (several mid-rolls).

## Rules for every video
- Title: an American underdog, a number, a clever tactic or a mistake, plus the year. No name-first titles.
- Long videos: 20–25 min, 16:9, maps every 60–90 s, animated troop movements (the same 2D toolkit as the WWI Short).
- Shorts: 9:16, 35–55 s, one moment, end on a question; pin a link to the matching long video.
- Facts: hedge contested details ("reportedly", "accounts differ") and list sources in the description.
