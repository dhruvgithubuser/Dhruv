## MON 16: The Pearl Harbor quote Yamamoto never said
**Cover text:** A MOVIE WROTE IT. The title carries a date and a name; the cover doesn't repeat it.
**Hook options (hookscore):**
1. ★ "You know the most famous quote about Pearl Harbor. Nobody can find it before 1970." (**68, workable**, the best of 15 drafts)
2. "The admiral who planned Pearl Harbor told Japan exactly how it would lose. Why didn't anyone listen?" (47)

**Script (about 55 s):**
> You know the most famous quote about Pearl Harbor. Nobody can find it before 1970.
> "I fear all we have done is to awaken a sleeping giant, and fill him with a terrible resolve."
> It's credited to Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto, the man who planned the attack. It's on posters, in documentaries, in classrooms.
> But there is no evidence he ever said it. The line comes from a movie: *Tora! Tora! Tora!*, released in 1970.
> The real Yamamoto had studied at Harvard and served in Washington. He had seen American factories.
> Before the war, he wrote a private letter: to win, Japan would have to march into Washington and dictate peace in the White House. Then he asked if Japan's politicians were ready for that sacrifice.
> The letter spread without that last sentence. In America, it read like a boast. It was a warning.
> The quote he never said made him a prophet. The words he did write made him a villain.
> Which version did you learn? Follow Buried Chronicles.

4. a young Japanese officer at Harvard, 1920;

6. a handwritten letter with the last line torn off;
7. a newspaper headline;
8. Yamamoto at his desk, looking out to sea.

## TUE 17: Napoleon's coffin opened
**Cover text:** HE LOOKED ASLEEP. Title lint: 98/100.
**Hook options (hookscore):**
1. ★ "You'd think 19 years in the ground leaves only bones. Then they opened Napoleon's coffin." (46)
2. "Why did Napoleon's body look almost untouched 19 years after his death?" (46)

**Script (about 55 s):**
> You'd think 19 years in the ground leaves only bones. Then they opened Napoleon's coffin.
> October 1840. Saint Helena, a rock in the South Atlantic. By torchlight, British soldiers dig up Napoleon Bonaparte, so France can bring him home.
> Under the stone: four coffins, one inside another. When the last lid is lifted, the witnesses see only a white shape. They fear the worst.
> Then the ship's surgeon rolls back a sheet of satin that had fallen from the lid.
> Napoleon's face is instantly recognisable. Eyes closed. Eyelashes still there. His green uniform almost perfect. The red ribbon of the Legion of Honour still across his chest.
> Nineteen years after his death, the Emperor looked like he was asleep.
> Two months later, Paris carried him to Les Invalides, where he lies today.
> How did his body survive? Some blamed arsenic. Others, four sealed coffins. What do you think? Follow Buried Chronicles.
