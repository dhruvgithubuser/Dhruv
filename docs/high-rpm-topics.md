# High-RPM US documentary topics (2D style): research, Nov 2026

## Method
- RPM itself is private (only the channel owner sees it in YouTube Studio). High RPM comes from the advertisers a topic
  attracts: finance, investing, banking, gold/bullion, insurance, legal and health pay the most. So the shortlist is
  US-history stories that sit inside those categories.
- Demand was then measured on live YouTube data (yt-dlp, read-only): 24 candidate topics, ~1,100 search results,
  filtered to on-topic videos of 4 min+, each compared with its own channel's median views ("multiple").
- Typical public RPM ranges (creator-reported, not measured here): finance/investing long-form with a US audience
  ≈ $8–20+; business ≈ $6–12; general history / true crime ≈ $3–6. **Shorts pay far less (≈ $0.03–0.10 per 1,000
  views)**. The high RPM only comes from long-form, 8+ minutes so mid-roll ads can run.

### Bench (strong, but weaker on RPM or crowded)
- **2008 / Lehman Brothers**: FD Finance 1.4M (84x), How Business Worked 2.3M. High RPM, but crowded.
- **Charles Ponzi (1920)**: MagnatesMedia 4.6M, Biographics 30x. Pairs well with Madoff.
- **Chicago Tylenol murders (1982)**: Biographics 52x, Retro Report 109x. Pharma/health ads.
- **FTX**: crypto ads pay well, but the story is recent, with legal sensitivity.
- **D.B. Cooper**: huge demand (BuzzFeed 23.9M, 88x), but mystery content earns mid RPM and LEMMiNO owns it.
- Weak right now: the savings & loan crisis, LTCM and Black Monday 1987 (little demand on YouTube).

## How to turn this into high RPM
1. Make each topic as an **8–12 minute 16:9 long-form** (the high-RPM product), then cut 2–3 Shorts from it that point to the long video.
2. Start with the gap topics, **#2 (EO 6102) and #4 (Silver Thursday)**: proven demand, few competitors, gold/silver advertisers.
3. Keep the "one moment" framing from your Shorts ("the first 6 hours", "the day…", "the night…").
   In this data it beats name-led titles.
