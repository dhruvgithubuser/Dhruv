# Four Men Could Lift a Sherman Tank: The Ghost Army

**Format:** YouTube Short / Instagram Reel, 9:16, about 62 s
**Style:** Vox-style explainer. Real public-domain US Army photos used as collage cut-outs, with yellow highlighter, hand-drawn circles and arrows, flat vector maps, kinetic type and data graphics. **No AI paintings.**
**Voice:** deep American documentary male.

---

## Fact sheet

| Fact | Value used | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | 23rd Headquarters Special Troops ("Ghost Army"), about 1,100 men, activated 20 Jan 1944 | Wikipedia *Ghost Army*; Ghost Army Legacy Project |
| Mission | Make the enemy misjudge the size and location of Allied forces, with a "traveling road show" of inflatable tanks, sound trucks and fake radio traffic | Wikipedia *Ghost Army* |
| Who | Many were recruited from art schools and advertising agencies: artists, architects, actors, set designers. Members included Bill Blass, Ellsworth Kelly, Arthur Singer and Art Kane. | Wikipedia *Ghost Army*; Smithsonian |
| Cyclist anecdote | Two French cyclists saw four GIs pick up a Sherman tank. A GI reportedly replied, "The Americans are very strong." Accounts differ on the details (Normandy, 1944). | Smithsonian (veteran Arthur Shilstone); War on the Rocks |
| Dummy tank | About **93 lb**. Rubber-tube frame under rubberized canvas, made by U.S. Rubber and Goodyear. A real M4 Sherman weighs roughly 66,000 lb (about 33 tons). | Smithsonian / Beyer & Sayles; worksthatwork.com; M4 Sherman specs |
| Visual unit | 603rd Camouflage Engineers (379 men): inflatable tanks, trucks, artillery and airplanes, set up in a few hours | Wikipedia; worksthatwork.com |
| Sound | 3132nd Signal Service Company Special. Bell Labs helped record armor and infantry at Fort Knox; half-track loudspeakers could be heard about **15 miles** away | Wikipedia; Hackaday; War History Online |
| Radio | "Spoof radio": operators imitated the traffic of real units | War History Online |
| Operations | More than 20 deceptions, from Normandy to the Rhine | Wikipedia |
| Viersen | 18–24 Mar 1945. They simulated the 30th and 79th Infantry Divisions with 600+ inflatables and spoof radio. The real Ninth Army crossed to the north on the night of 23 March and met little resistance. Lt. Gen. William Simpson commended the 23rd. | Ghost Army Legacy Project (official history); National WWII Museum; History.com |
| Secrecy | Kept secret until **1996** | Wikipedia |
| Honors | Ghost Army Congressional Gold Medal Act signed **1 Feb 2022**; medal presented 21 Mar 2024 | Wikipedia; C-SPAN |
| Not used | The "15,000–30,000 lives saved" estimate is from a single unattributed source, so I left it out. | — |

## Script

| # | Narration | On screen (Vox treatment) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In 1944, two French cyclists watched four American soldiers pick up a Sherman tank. | Flat illustration: two cyclists, four GI icons lifting a Sherman silhouette. Tag "FRANCE, 1944". |
| 2 | One soldier reportedly shrugged: "Americans are very strong." | A speech bubble types out, with a "reportedly" source tag. |
| 3 | The tank was a balloon. It weighed ninety-three pounds. | The archival dummy-Sherman photo drops in. Weight bars: real Sherman ≈ 66,000 lb vs 93 lb, with an air-valve callout. |
| 4 | It belonged to the Ghost Army: eleven hundred Americans whose job was to look like thirty thousand. | A unit chart of 1,100 figures multiplies to 30,000. Tag "23RD HEADQUARTERS SPECIAL TROOPS". |
| 5 | Many were artists, recruited from art schools and ad agencies. | Sketchbook cards and name tags: Bill Blass, Ellsworth Kelly, Arthur Singer, Art Kane. |
| 6 | They inflated fake tanks, trucks and cannons in a few hours. | Inflatable icons puff up from flat; a clock runs. |
| 7 | Sound trucks blasted recordings of tank columns that carried fifteen miles. Radio operators faked the chatter of entire divisions. | A half-track with a speaker sends out sound rings labelled "15 MILES"; a radio mast with Morse dots. |
| 8 | From Normandy to the Rhine, they staged more than twenty deceptions. | Map of France → Germany with a route line and a counter 0 → 20+. |
| 9 | The biggest came in March 1945. Along the Rhine, they set up more than six hundred dummies and impersonated two divisions. | Rhine map zooms in; dummy-tank icons fill the Viersen sector; fake "30th" and "79th" division badges. |
| 10 | The Germans took the bait. To the north, the real Ninth Army crossed the river and met little resistance. | German arrows turn toward the fakes; the real crossing arrow goes in at Rheinberg. The Rhine-crossing photo card and General Simpson. |
| 11 | The unit stayed secret until 1996. In 2022, they were awarded the Congressional Gold Medal. | A "TOP SECRET" folder is stamped "DECLASSIFIED 1996", then a gold medal icon with "2022". |
| 12 | An army of artists, fighting with balloons. Would you have fallen for it? | The dummy tank card returns with a question mark. End tag. |
