# One Wrong Turn That Started World War I: script + image/video prompts

## Full voiceover script (79.7 s)
One of history's deadliest wars almost never happened. A wrong turn, a stalled engine, and two gunshots changed the world forever.

June 28, 1914. Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, rides through Sarajevo with his wife, Sophie. Along the route, six assassins are waiting.

At ten past ten, one of them throws a bomb. It bounces off the folded roof and explodes under the next car, wounding around twenty people. The Archduke is unharmed. The plot has failed.

At the town hall, he is furious: "I came here on a visit, and I am greeted with bombs!" He decides to visit the wounded in hospital, driving straight along the river. But no one tells his driver.

At the Latin Bridge, the cars ahead turn right, and his driver follows. The governor shouts: "Wrong way!" The driver brakes, tries to reverse, and the engine stalls.

Standing five feet away is 19-year-old Gavrilo Princip. He had missed his chance that morning. Now his target is right in front of him. He steps forward and fires twice.

The first bullet strikes the Archduke. The second strikes Sophie. Within the hour, both are dead.

One month later, Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia. Alliances pull in Russia, Germany, France and Britain. An estimated 20 million people would die in the war that followed. All because of one wrong turn.
